# DLC specials — `dlc.html`

> Part of the Distrilight tools suite — start from `CLAUDE.md` in the repo root, which
> holds the hard rules, the map of the repo and how to check a change. This file is
> the depth for one tool: read it before you change `dlc.html`.

`dlc.html` is the **DLC specials** tool: paste a supplier's specification text for a fixture that is *not* in the product data, add photos, and it produces a DLC product sheet. Unlike the rest of the suite this one carries the **DLC** house style, taken from the supplied design (`DLC Productblad.indd`, example Norma Track) — a black header band with the DLC wordmark, Poppins for the headings and Helvetica for the tables. Every measurement in the `M` table at the top of `bouwBlad()` was read out of that PDF rather than eyeballed, and `y` there is a **baseline**, not a box top (`tekst()` converts) — that is what keeps the measured numbers usable as-is.

**The photos go in five slots** (`BEELDVAKKEN`, numbered as in the user's sketch of September 2026) laid out in **two zones on one grid** (`hoofdfotoPlek()`, `onderzoneBoven()`, `onderzone()`; the numbers in `FOTO`, all measured on the sheet). **Top zone:** the specifications left, 1 Hoofdfoto right beside them. **Bottom zone:** everything else, always *under* the specifications and under photo 1 — 3 Toepassing left, 4 Detail 1 and 5 Detail 2 side by side on the right with 2 Afmetingen under them. The bottom zone starts on one line across the page, so 3 and 4|5 always start together: one gutter under photo 1 (the right column then reads as one evenly spaced stack 1, 4|5, 2) or 24 pt under the table's last rule, whichever is lower. The grid: two columns, the table (35.4–288.4) and the photo column from one column gap further to the right edge of the DLC logo (323.8–559.9); that gap equals the left margin and the logo edge sits at the same margin from the right, so the page is symmetric. Photo 3 sits exactly under the table; 1, 4|5 and 2 exactly under each other. Photo 1 starts at the cap height of BESTELGEGEVENS (141), so image and data begin on one line. Each photo gets the full column width and the height its own shape gives it, with one gutter (12 pt) between photos, also between 4 and 5. Too tall: the hoofdfoto and the drawing get narrower and centred (nothing is cut off a product or a dimension drawing), unless the hoofdfoto is only just too tall (≤ 8 %, a 3:4 portrait), where losing some background beats edges 7 pt off those below; the details and the toepassing are cropped (`metUitsnede` + `beeldVullend`). 4 and 5 are twins — same width and height, the height following their average shape within 4:5 portrait to 4:3 landscape. The bottom of 3 lands on the bottom of 2 (or of 4|5) when that costs at most a third of cropping and keeps it no taller than square, so the zone closes as one block — a landscape 3:2 beside 4|5 + 2 needs 26–30 %, which is why the limit is a third and not a quarter. If the bottom zone no longer fits on the page (the drawing may shrink by a quarter first), it moves **as a whole** to the top of a new page rather than breaking across two. `controleer-logica.mjs` checks the grid on the real functions, including a sweep over 29,160 combinations of table length and photo shapes (3 and 4|5 start together, nothing touches the table, photo 1 or the footer, 3 stays under the table). The panel shows the five slots in the same arrangement; an image is set by clicking (file picker), Ctrl+V or dropping a file on the slot, and **dragging an image onto another slot swaps the two**. A file dropped next to a slot is swallowed rather than letting the browser open it and leave the special. **Older specials** had a hoofdfoto plus a row of images at the bottom: `beeldenNaarVakken()` moves that row, in legend order, into the empty slots 2–5 when the special is opened, and whatever does not fit stays in `S.beelden`, shown under "Nog niet geplaatst" to drag into a slot or remove — it is not drawn on the sheet. `controleer-logica.mjs` checks the migration and the swap on the real code.

**The user steers what is in a slot, never the grid** (`S.uitsnede`, `uitsnedeNorm()`, `uitsneden()`, `uitsnedeMaat()`; the controls in `uitsnedeBewerker()`). The automatic layout sometimes crops what the user wants to show — measured: up to 36 % of a portrait toepassing, 25–39 % of a detail whose shape differs from its twin, always centred — so every filled slot has a ✂ button that opens a crop editor under the five slots: the slot in its real shape from the last drawing (`kaders`, filled by `bouwBlad()`), the photo in it as it will land on the sheet, and the part that falls off shown dimmed around it. Four controls. **Verschuiven** (drag, or the arrow keys, Shift for bigger steps) picks which part stays in the slot. **Inzoomen** (100–300 %, also + and −) comes with a sharpness readout (≈ dpi on the sheet, red below 150). **Hele foto tonen** cuts nothing: the slot takes the photo's shape within the same grid and edges — photo 1 gets narrower and stays centred; 3 keeps the table width, or as a portrait gets narrower, left under the table, with its bottom on a bottom on the right when that costs at most a third; 4|5 become one row of equal height whose widths follow their shapes and add up to exactly the column (4 on the left edge, 5 on the right, at most as tall as the column is wide), which is why heel on 4 or 5 applies to the pair. 2 Afmetingen is always whole and has no heel switch. **Automatisch** puts the slot back to what the tool does by itself. Pan and zoom never move or resize a slot: x and y are clamped to 0–1 (object-position semantics — point x of the photo lands on point x of the slot) and zoom to 1–3, so the slot is always covered, and the values are rounded to 1/10000. The crop belongs to the photo: a new photo in a slot, or emptying it, drops it (`plaatsBeeld()`); swapping two slots takes it along (`wisselBeelden()`); a photo dragged to the unplaced row loses it. A slot without a choice is absent from `S.uitsnede`, and then `bouwBlad()` takes exactly the old drawing path — five test sheets (every shape, a 3:4 hoofdfoto, the bottom zone moving to page 2) compare equal on content with the version before the controls (`tools/vergelijk.mjs`). The filled slots in the panel show each photo as it lands on the sheet (`werkMiniaturenBij()`: a small JPEG in the slot's shape with the crop applied), so a cut-off edge shows without hunting through the preview. `controleer-logica.mjs` checks the clamping, the pair rule, that a photo always covers its slot (1,800 cases), the heel layouts, that without heel the grid is exactly the old one and with everything heel nothing is cropped and every slot has its photo's shape (104,976 combinations each), and that the crop follows the photo; each of those checks was made to fail once on purpose.

The header band is a JPEG lifted from the supplied PDF and baked in as `merk/dlc-kopbalk.jpg` → `MERK_BEELDEN.dlcKopbalk`. The catalogue is the fifty fields the suppliers we buy from actually publish, in six groups — the four of the supplied design plus `Algemeen` (brand, category, EAN, packaging, lamp holder) and `Sensoren`. **A field with no value is left off the sheet, and a group whose fields are all empty disappears with its heading**, so a sparse special still comes out looking like the reference. Field groups are fixed (that is what makes the sheet recognisable); within a group fields can be emptied, renamed or added. The DLC tool has its **own** `VELDMAP` — it shares the reader with the vergelijker but not the field map — so a label spelling added in one does not reach the other; keep the two in step when a supplier turns up a new wording (the housing colour is the worked example: `kleur behuizing` / `behuizingskleur` / `kastkleur` / `housing colour` / `body colour` / `finish`). **An empty field is left off the sheet, and a group whose fields are all empty disappears with its heading.** A special is a one-off, so "Special opslaan" saves it the same way `armaturenboek.html`/`index.html`/`vergelijking.html` save a project: it bakes the current state into a self-contained copy of `dlc.html` itself (`slaSpecialOp()`), which reopens fully filled in when double-clicked — there is no separate "Openen" button or JSON file. The saved copy leaves out the panel and the preview: the tool rebuilds both from `S` when it opens, and in the file they were only a second copy of every photo (a third with the crop editor open) and a `blob:` address that cannot load from disk — measured with five test photos, 1,073 kB → 584 kB, and no console error on opening.
