# Bandrasters — `bandrasters.html`

> Part of the Distrilight tools suite — start from `CLAUDE.md` in the repo root, which
> holds the hard rules, the map of the repo and how to check a change. This file is
> the depth for one tool: read it before you change `bandrasters.html`.

`bandrasters.html` is the **Bandrasters** tool: a per-position intake for bandraster (grid-ceiling) replacement projects — the existing fixture on the left, the Distrilight alternative on the right — that yields two deliverables, a **technical product sheet** for the customer and an **RFQ list** for the manufacturer who has to build the alternatives.

It replaces `Bandraster_Intake_Nieuw_V3.xlsx`. Everything that lived on that workbook's hidden `Datasheet` sheet (the dropdown lists, the lumen table per TL tube × CRI/colour temperature, the optic efficiencies) is now the data tables at the top of the script; the `Formulier` formulas are the calculation layer, and the English `Bestelformulier` columns are the RFQ. The workbook had three fixed intake sheets; here the number of positions is free. Three things deliberately differ from it, each with a comment saying why: **5000K is not offered** (the lumen table only has columns for 2700/3000/4000/6500K per CRI, so 5000K always ran into "Gegevens Ontbreken"); the control/connector dependency was keyed on an explicit `groep` field rather than on the spelling of the control name (the workbook's list said `Aan-uit` while its formulas tested `Aan-Uit`, leaving the connector list empty for two of the five controls) — that dependency is gone since the November 2026 lists (see below); and the RFQ carries the **alternative's** housing colour, where `Bestelformulier!P21` pointed at the existing fixture's.

The calculation is the workbook's, verified against its worked example (project 7633: 1× TL5-35W behind Prisma at 4000K/80+ → 2230 lm existing, 16 W and 2130 lm for a microprismatic alternative): existing lumen = tubes × lumen(tube, CRI+colour temp) × old optic efficiency; then wattage = `floor(lm / new optic efficiency / efficacy)` capped at a maximum, and new lumen = W × efficacy × new optic efficiency. Efficacy (140 lm/W) and the cap (60 W) live in `P.instellingen` rather than as constants, so the tool can move with a better LED generation without a code change — but they are **no longer shown**: neither as fields in the project card nor as a Systeemrendement row on the product sheet. Bringing them back is two inputs bound to `P.instellingen` (see the note in `bindProject`).

**Under 640px the intake folds**: existing | field name | alternative does not fit side by side, so the field name moves above the left box (`data-veld` on both cells, set in `tekenIntake()`, shown through `::before`; invisible on the right so both boxes start at the same height) and the two boxes keep half the width each. The single-column werkblad below 1180px is `minmax(0,1fr)`, not `1fr` — a `1fr` column is never narrower than its widest content, and the intake stretched the whole page to 606px on a 390px phone. Nothing changes on a laptop. The right-hand column mirrors the left until you overrule a field — `volgt[id] === false` marks it loose, `rechts()` is the single place that resolves which value applies, and a `↺` button re-couples it. A connector that does not exist in the list belonging to the chosen control counts as *not filled in* rather than being silently cleared, so it cannot slip into the RFQ after a control change or after reopening an older project.

The **product sheet** carries the **DLC** house style (same `M` measurement table as `dlc.html`, same baseline convention) but with field groups of its own — Algemeen, Afmetingen, Lichttechniek, Elektrisch. Empty fields and empty groups drop out, as in `dlc.html`. It is a sheet about the product, not about the job: what the alternative replaces and which project it is for stay in the tool and in the RFQ. Artikelcode falls back to **Special** when no product code is filled in, since a bandraster alternative is a one-off. The position's own **Bestekcode** — a field at the top of the intake card, next to the position description, not a row in the grid — is stamped into the header band, at the armaturenboek's stamp geometry (27 pt down, 11 pt, right-aligned, extra codes stacked 13 pt apart, shrinking rather than shifting when too wide) — with two deliberate differences, both forced by the artwork: it ends 155 pt from the right edge instead of 145.4, because the diagonal edge of the band's white wedge sits at x=445..453 at that height, and it is drawn in the sheet's ink rather than white, because it lands on that white wedge. `M.stempel` holds those numbers; a `;` in the field splits it into several stamped lines. At the foot of the sheet it repeats the alternative's optic and connector photo, side by side with a hairline and caption in the table's own style — no heading above them; the captions say what they are. The block is pinned above the footer line rather than following the table, so a short sheet does not leave it floating in the middle; when the table runs into that area the block moves to a new page (`M.foto`, at the end of `bouwProductblad`). It draws with the shared layer's own `sluitBeeldIn`/`beeldPassend`, so a photo pasted in as PNG works alongside the generated JPEGs. Instead of a photo it carries a **generated dimension drawing**: the housing (l×b×h) with its light apertures in it, as a top view and a side elevation with dimension lines. `figuurVormen()` computes the shapes once and `figuurSvg()` / `figuurPdf()` render the same list on screen and in the PDF, so the preview cannot drift from the sheet. Dimension arrows flip to the outside when a span is too short to hold them.

A position can have **more than one light aperture, and they need not be centred** — `nieuw.lichtvlakken` is a list of `{l, b, x, y}`, where `x`/`y` are the distance from the left and top edge in the top view. Every field is optional: what is left blank `lichtvlakken()` derives — `RAND` (15 mm) around, the length split evenly over n apertures, and whatever length is left over spread across the n+1 gaps — so emptying a field is also how you hand it back to the tool, and the input's placeholder shows what it then computes. A value that would put an aperture outside the housing is clamped **for the drawing only**; the typed number stays. The drawing keeps its old single dimension line for the common case (one aperture, centred) and switches to a **chain** (edge, aperture, gap, aperture, edge) as soon as there are several or one sits off-centre, so the numbers add up to the length; a segment too narrow for its own number gets only the arrows, which is why `figuurVormen()` takes the renderer's font size. In that same case each aperture also gets its **distance from the top edge**, as a short vertical dimension inside the top view at the aperture's own x — `vertikaal(…, {binnen:true})`, which keeps a short dimension inside its own span instead of letting the arrows stick out, because these start on the edge of the view where the view name sits.

The aperture fields live **under the figure**, in the "Afgeleid figuur" card, not as a row in the intake grid: you type dimensions for something you can see beside them, and the same aperture can be dragged in the drawing (`tekenLichtvlakken()`, called from `tekenAlles()` after `tekenIntake()` has cleared `AFGELEID`).

On screen the apertures are **draggable, one axis at a time, in 5 mm steps** (`SLEEPSTAP`; typing stays free to the millimetre, and the step is applied before the clamp so an aperture can still sit flush against an edge that is not a multiple of 5). Each carries a small two-arrow gizmo drawn by `figuurSvg()` — not by `figuurVormen()`, so it stays off the product sheet, where it would read as part of the drawing. Each axis is its own `<g class="as">` so CSS can scale it on `:hover`, which is how you see which way you are about to pull; the axis being dragged carries `actief`, rendered from `sleep` rather than set as a class afterwards, because the figure is redrawn on every pointermove. Pulling an arrow moves only along that axis; dragging the body picks the axis from the first 3 px of movement, so nothing ever drifts diagonally. The x hit area is **split into two arms** with the crossing point left to y: one rect for both would mean the centre silently belongs to whichever was drawn last. The "centreren" button in each aperture's header empties `x` and `y`, which is the same as never having filled them in — back to the calculated position.

The mechanics: `figuurVormen()` returns `{vormen, kader}`, where `kader` is the px↔mm mapping of the top view, and `figuurSvg()` stores it in `figuurKader` (the SVG renderer, not `figuurVormen` itself — a PDF export would otherwise overwrite it with the sheet's own scale) and marks each aperture rect with `data-vlak`, each grip with `data-as`. The pointer handlers hang on the `<svg>` rather than on the rects, which are replaced on every redraw, and use `getScreenCTM()` so the page's own scaling does not matter. A drag writes rounded millimetres into `x`/`y` — the same thing typing does, so a dragged aperture is simply one with those fields filled in. `ververs()` copies the state back into the input values (not just the placeholders) so the fields follow the drag without rebuilding the grid. A project saved before this carried `nieuw.lichtvlakL`/`lichtvlakB`; `herstelConcept()` moves those into the first aperture and drops the old keys. Which base product a position is built on is a field (since November 2026 the four families below, or a typed-in one); the exact sheet layout is still being worked out.

The **RFQ list** is Distrilight-branded and English, in two forms: a `.xlsx` with the `Bestelformulier` columns for the manufacturer to price, and a PDF (cover-less: header block, summary table, then a spec block per position). `Product Code` and `Unit Price` are editable in the tool as well as in the exported sheet. The `.xlsx` is written by hand — `zipMaak()` builds a store-only (uncompressed) zip so a CRC32 is all the machinery needed, and cells use inline strings so there is no shared-string table. **Don't add a spreadsheet library for this**; the file is one sheet without formulas.

"Project opslaan" uses the same self-baking pattern as `dlc.html`/`armaturenboek.html`: state is baked into a copy of `bandrasters.html` that reopens filled in.

**Photos.** Under the ceiling type, the base family, the optic, the connector and the plenum the form shows a thumbnail (click to enlarge) — you cannot tell from a name alone which ceiling, which profile or which connector you are picking — and the product sheet carries the optic and connector photos at the bottom. They come from `window.BANDRASTER_BEELDEN` in `bandraster-beelden.js` in the repo root: `{plafond:{naam:dataURI}, basis:{…}, optiek:{…}, aansluiting:{…}, plenum:{…}}`, data URIs for the same tainted-canvas reason as `armatuur-beelden.js`. A missing file is not an error: the form simply shows none. `beeldVoor()` looks a photo up by the name itself (the normalised form only absorbs case and spaces); for a connector `aansluitFotoNaam()` builds the name — per choice, per plug of a cord ("Snoer Schuko") and per brand of a plug-in solution ("Stekerbaar Wieland"). Photos are stripped from the DOM before "Project opslaan" bakes it: they already live in `bandraster-beelden.js`, which the saved copy reaches through its `<base href>`.

**The final set (13 November 2026).** The user supplied `bandraster-beelden.js` as it now is, apart from its header comment: 25 photos of 900 px for 31 of the 33 names the form can ask for — a room photo with a profile inset for each of the seven ceiling types, a strip for EU (Opaal/Microprismatisch) and Flexcore and a louvre for EU (Ledil) and Optic, the optics, the GST18i3/i4/i5 chassis, the two cords, the logos of InstalinQ, Adels and Wieland, and both plenum versions. "A-symmetrisch" and "Geen (direct driver)" have none, and that is how the set was delivered ("dit zijn de foto's zoals ze zijn"). It was put together with the tool's own **"Foto's" card** at the bottom of the page (a slot per name, filled from a file or with Ctrl+V, and "Beeldbestand opslaan" wrote the file); with the photos final the card was removed (the user: "hierna mogen die foto vakken eronder ook gesloten worden"). It is in the git history up to `128610c`, should it be needed again. Removing it also closed a leak: "Project opslaan" emptied only the card's optic and connector slots before baking the page, so its ceiling, base and plenum slots — twelve photos — went into every saved project (345 kB with the provisional photos, and 1041 kB it would have been with the final ones). A saved project now carries none (147 kB). `controleer-logica.mjs` takes over the card's counter: every name the form can show a photo for has one except those two (at least 31, which may only go up), every photo in the file belongs to such a name (a renamed choice would otherwise lose its photo without a sound, leaving the old name behind as an orphan), and every photo is a JPEG or PNG of at most 900 px — `sluitBeeldIn()` embeds nothing else in a PDF. Each of those was made to fail once: a renamed key, a removed photo, a WebP, a 2000-px original, a renamed ceiling type. To replace a photo, replace its data URI in the file.

Until then `bandraster-beelden.js` was generated by `tools/maak-bandraster-beelden.mjs` from `Bandraster_Intake_Nieuw_V3.xlsx` and the November list. That script is gone — run once more, it would have put the provisional renders back over the final photos — and is in the git history up to `128610c`. What it knew is worth keeping: in-cell images are not floating drawings — the cell carries a `vm="12"` that runs through `xl/metadata.xml` → `xl/richData/rdrichvalue.xml` → `richValueRel.xml` to a file in `xl/media/`, and the hidden Datasheet pairs those image columns with the name columns, row for row (`AA→AE` and `AD→AE` for the optics, `AH→AI` and `AK→AL` for the connectors — the same columns the Formulier's own XLOOKUPs in E23/L23/E28/L28 use); the list's images are floating drawings, found by their anchor cell in `xl/drawings/drawing1.xml`. And JPEG on white rather than PNG: the renders came on a transparent background, but they sit on a white card anyway, and PNG made the file seven times bigger.

## The November 2026 lists (draft)

Inside sales put what was still missing in a list (`docs/bronnen/bandraster_lijst.xlsx`, 6 November 2026), and it **replaces** the old workbook's choices (the user, 9 November: "vervangen"). It is a first version to go over with the team; part of the content still has to come from colleagues — final dimensions, specifications and way of working from Quenn and Elise, wattage and lumen per family from R&D (the photos came on 13 November) — and until then the tool says "volgt" or "indicatief" instead of guessing. The workbook itself is not in the repo as it came: it carried a colleague's name, work e-mail, Microsoft account id and OneDrive path. `python3 tools/schoon-werkboek.py <bron> <doel>` blanks exactly those (author, the person list behind the comments, the path) and leaves content, layout, images and comment text — run it again on a new version.

- **Ceiling types** (`PLAFONDTYPE`): Lay-On Ceiling and Opbouw (Standaard), Clip-In Metal, Hook-Over Metal, Hook-On Metal, Lay-In and Gypsum Board Ceiling (Special; "Gypsun" in the list), plus `Anders:`. Special shows as a label in the intake and as "(special)" in the RFQ's new **Ceiling type** column, not on the customer's product sheet. An old project's ceiling type becomes `Anders:` with its old name.
- **Base families** (`BASIS`, the list's "optiek"): EU (Opaal/Microprismatisch), EU (Ledil), Flexcore, Optic. The optic choice below stays (`OPTIEK_NIEUW`), because the lumen calculation runs on its efficiency. The base row moved up, above the lamp: together they decide the strips. Old `Optic bandraster`/`Flexcore bandraster` become Optic/Flexcore.
- **Strip dimensions** (`LENGTEKLASSE`, `STROOK_RIJEN`, `STROOK`): the list's table "Bestaande lamp met afmetingen nieuw raster" — per old tube length (60/90/120/150 cm) and number of tubes, per family the strip width A and length B — in the list's sketch A is the short side of one strip and B its long side, so "2x40mm" are two strips of 40 mm wide side by side. The tool always writes the length first, with the letters beside it ("2 stroken, elk 1068 mm lang (B) en 86 mm breed (A)", strip fields "lengte (B)" and "breedte (A)"), as it writes every size (length × width); the first draft said "86 × 1068" in two places, which read as if A and B had been swapped (the user: "kijk ook goed wat A en B de afmetingen betekenen"). The numbers themselves were right: all 68 cells of the table (17 rows × 4 families) come out of the tool as they are in the list. More tubes are more strips side by side in the width, except Flexcore, which stays at 2 strips of 86 mm for 3 and 4 tubes. `strookVoorstel(c)` returns `{a, b, n, nTabel}` or `{reden}`: EU (Opaal/Microprismatisch) is empty in the list ("volgt via R&D"), and a combination the table does not have (3 × 36W, 2 × 21W) gets no proposal. **Aantal stroken** (`c.nieuw.stroken`, empty = the table) may be lower — "Kunnen 2/3/4 oude T8/T5 in een strook zijn". With a proposal, `lichtvlakken()` gives n strips of A × B side by side in the width, centred in the length, with equal gaps (`strookVlakken()`); typed or dragged values win as before, and without a proposal nothing changed. The strip fields lose their + and × (the count comes from Aantal stroken). The drawing then shows a chain across the width (edge, strip, gap, strip, edge) to the right of the top view, with the overall width one column further out, instead of a "from the top" dimension per strip; the length chain takes each boundary once.
- **Minimum space**: "Minimale ruimte A is 30mm per kant" for EU Opaal and Flexcore; for EU Ledil and Optic the list says "??". `strookRuimte(c)` warns, under the strip fields, when the strips with 30 mm around each strip do not fit the width (two Flexcore strips of 86 mm need 262 mm). Whether the 30 mm holds around each strip or only at the outer edges is an open point; nothing blocks on it.
- Under the strip fields: where the strips come from, "Optic: modules van 280 mm (7 leds)", "815 mm staat in de lijst met R&D check" for Flexcore at 90 cm, and the list's own note — "Deze afmetingen zijn indicatief en niet definitief. Na opdracht worden de definitieve afmetingen vastgelegd." (`MATEN_INDICATIEF`), which is also an Opmerking row under Afmetingen on the product sheet. The computed watt and lumen say "indicatief — wattage en lumen per basisarmatuur volgen via R&D"; the calculation itself is unchanged.
- **Control** (`STURING`): Aan-Uit, DALI, 1-10V, PushDim, Fase dimbaar, Draadloos Casambi, Draadloos Philips, Halfdraadloos Helvar, Anders: (typed). Old Pushdim-Switchdim becomes PushDim, Aan-Uit+Dipswitch becomes Anders: with that name.
- **Follow-up questions** (`VERVOLG`): connector — Geen (direct driver), GST18i3/i4/i5 chassis, Stekerbaar (brand InstalinQ/Adels/Wieland/Anders → cores 2/3/4/5/5+2 BST → fire class Eca/Dca/Cca/B2ca), Snoer (Schuko/Eurosnoer → length in m); sensor — Nee/Ja → integrated/external → wired/wireless → motion/daylight/both; emergency — Geen/Centraal/Decentraal → manual/self-test → 1/3 hours → DALI; plenum — Nee/Ja → Standaard/Special → what the special is. Each step is a key of its own in `oud`/`nieuw`, so mirroring and ↺ work per step; `tekenVervolg()` draws the steps that the answers so far call for, and a hidden step counts nowhere (`vervolgMist()`). `aansluitTekst()`, `sensorTekst()`, `noodTekst()` and `plenumTekst()` give the product sheet's Dutch and the RFQ's English; the RFQ's Air slot column is now **Plenum**.
- **Old projects** (`herstelKeuzes()`, from `herstelConcept()`): converted where it is unambiguous — Kaal → Geen, GST18-3/-5 male chassis → GST18i3/i5, a Euro or RA (Schuko) cord of N m → Snoer with that plug and length, Sensor Daglicht/Beweging → Ja with that kind, the nine emergency options → test, duration and DALI (central or decentral the old list never said), ventilatie → plenum. What does not fit (a splitter, male + female, "+ kaal", BST14) stays visible as "was: …" under the empty question and counts as not filled in, so it cannot slip into the RFQ. A right side that was cut loose stays loose for every step of that question. Running it twice changes nothing.
- **Photos**: provisionally the list's own images — one per ceiling type, two shared ones for the families (EU Opaal and Flexcore a strip, EU Ledil and Optic the "5 optieken" image), the standard plenum — plus four old connector renders under the new name of the same part, until the final set came on 13 November (see Photos above).

`controleer-logica.mjs` checks the strip table against the list itself, row by row (every tube × every family, at least 96 cases, and no combination the list does not have), the examples above, the strip layout and a typed value winning, the 30 mm check, the width chain in the drawing, the follow-up texts in Dutch and English and in the RFQ columns, which steps count as open, and an old project converted and converted again; eleven deliberate mistakes are each caught.
