# Armatuurvergelijker — `vergelijking.html` (generated from `vergelijker/`)

> Part of the Distrilight tools suite — start from `CLAUDE.md` in the repo root, which
> holds the hard rules, the map of the repo and how to check a change. This file is
> the depth for one tool: read it before you change the vergelijker, its product data or the scripts in `vergelijker/`.

`vergelijking.html` is the **Armatuurvergelijker**: per bestekpositie it puts the installer's reference fixture next to the Distrilight alternative. The sheet's rows are `REF_VELDEN`, and that one table drives the on-screen sheet, the paste panel's labels **and** the PDF page, so adding a row is one entry. **`PDF_VERBORGEN` is the one exception**: Leverancier and Artikelnummer are on the sheet in the tool — inside sales has to be able to trace a position back — but they are left off the PDF, which goes outside. Neither column is printed, so the reference fixture's make (a bestek writes `Fabricaat`) and its article number stay in, and Pragmalux and our own article code stay off. What the fixture *is* still stands: the Type row names it and every technical row is untouched. A key in that list that is not a real `REF_VELDEN` row hides nothing and would go unnoticed, so `controleer-logica.mjs` asserts it is one. **Between filling in the reference and choosing the article sits a round past the manager** (`bouwAkkoord()`, the block above the picker): inside sales writes down what she has in mind as the alternative (`voorstel`, free text — “Sigma DALI”), the manager sets `akkoord` to ja or nee and, on a nee, writes in `reactie` which family it should be instead. Only then is the article number filled in, because only then is it settled which family to look in. **The exchange is “Project opslaan”** — that already bakes the whole state into a copy of the page, so the manager opens the same file, fills in his part, saves and mails it back; no server, no second file format, nothing to retype, and he has the full reference column under his nose, which is what it takes to judge whether the alternative fits. `PROJECT_VERSIE` went to 2 for those three fields: an older file opens fine (the restore tops them up) and a newer file in an older copy of the tool gets the warning, which is right — there the manager's verdict would be invisible. **The verdict can be searched in one click**: on a ✗ the reactie usually names the family it should be (“nee, dit moet de Mondial verdiept worden”), on a ✓ the voorstel does, so `reeksZoekterm()` takes the first word that points at a series — a word from a family name that is not a kind, colour or brand word (`ARM_TYPEWOORDEN`/`ARM_STOPWOORDEN`), nor a shape word that can only narrow (`GEEN_REEKSBEGIN`: “rond de 3000 lumen” is not about a family) — and keeps the words after it while the picker still finds something and no ordinary sentence word (`LOSSE_WOORDEN`) comes up; the button under the reactie puts that in the family picker as if typed (`zoekInKiezer()`), so the list opens and she still chooses herself. The rail carries a ✓/✗ per position and a “1 van 3 nog te beoordelen” line that only appears once the round is actually running, and the pre-export warnings name the positions that are rejected or still unassessed — a warning, not a block. **None of it reaches the PDF**, for the same reason as `PDF_VERBORGEN`: it is internal deliberation and the PDF goes outside. It is not a `REF_VELDEN` row at all, so nothing has to hide it, and `controleer-logica.mjs` asserts it stays that way. The right-hand column comes from `onzeWaarden(fam, v)` out of the product data; `ipVan()` is there because the IP rating sits in the data twice — as text on the family ("IP54 kamerzijde / IP20 achterzijde", the nuance you want to read on a comparison) and as a bare number on the variant — so the family's text wins while it agrees with the variant's number, and the variant wins when it doesn't, because that one is about this article. IK only exists on the family. `ugrVan()`/`criVan()` do the same for the two photometric-quality rows, and the sign is put in front of the number — the screen gets the real ≤/≥ and `pdfTxt()` turns those back into `<=`/`>=` for the PDF. **Both figures sit in the article's own description** (`UGR<19 CRI>90`) as well as on the family, and **the article wins**: for a long time neither was read out of the description at all, so those two rows were filled only for the families that happen to have a block in the hand-written overlay — the Sigma states `UGR<19` on every one of its 117 rows and still showed an empty UGR. Unlike `ipVan()` the two need no comparing, because the family figure carries no nuance the description lacks: both sides are a bare comparison, so precedence is enough. The sign is taken as written — `>90` is not `≥90`, and the overlay's `cri_min: 80` really is a lower bound, so the two render differently on purpose. A family without a UGR figure and an article that states none (a waterproof fixture has neither) yields nothing rather than a bare "UGR". The beam angle has no field of its own in the data; `bundelVan()` reads it out of the variant's `omschrijving` in Pragmalux' own notation (`… 1650lm 100D Ø150x80(H) …`) — only that shape, a number with a capital D and nothing attached to it, and only from the variant, because it is a property of the article. A family that does not state one yields nothing. `montageVan()` reads the mounting from the family's `montagewijzen` list, narrowed to the one the variant's `uitvoering` names — `standaard` and `verdiept` are recess depths, not mounting methods, so those leave the family's list standing. **Dimbaar and Aansturing are two rows, not one**: whether it can be dimmed, and with what. `aansturingVan()` writes the variant's `dimprotocol` the way an installer writes it (`dali2/1-10v/push-dim` → `DALI-2 / 1-10V / push-dim`, `geen` → `Aan/uit`) and `dimbaar` is only Ja/Nee. On the reference side a supplier usually names just one of the two and `naarBladvelden()` derives the other — a named dim protocol means Ja, `aan-uit` means Nee — but only when it really follows: `Aansturing: BM` leaves Dimbaar **empty**, because a motion sensor says nothing about dimming. `sensorVan()` and `noodVan()` pick the sensor and the emergency module out of the variant's description (`… + Bewegingssensor & Noodmodule 3uur zelftest`); a variant without one yields **empty, not "Nee"** — the data does not say there is none, it says nothing about it. The housing colour is the variant's own `kleur`, capitalised so it sits alongside a pasted `Signaalwit (RAL9003)` without looking like a typo. **Aansluiting has no source in the product data at all**, so that column stays empty and is typed by hand, like Toepassing. **Optiek and Rendement are rows on the sheet**, not fields that are merely kept: a comparison that leaves out the optic and the lm/W leaves out two of the things an installer actually weighs. Optiek comes off the variant (`optiek` — it is what separates the Mondial's facet reflector from its hoogglans one), and falls back to the **family** when the article says nothing: a Sigma or Essence panel is always prismatic but the price list writes that nowhere in the description, so it comes from the overlay, which is exactly what that layer is for. Says neither, then the row stays empty. Rendement is **not** in the price list and `rendementVan()` derives it: luminous flux divided by system wattage, which is a definition rather than a guess — as long as the two belong together. On a dipswitch fixture both are ranges and the ends pair up per setting; measured on this catalogue, 50-70W with 7279-10191lm gives 146 lm/W at both ends, so the ends are computed separately and become a range only when they differ after rounding. Miss either figure and the row stays empty. On the pasted side the same row needed a trim: `efficacy: 119lm/W, Ra80 typical` puts two statements in one sentence, and while there is no general way to cut there (the same cut would destroy `Paal, wand, sokkel of aardstaaf`), a rendement is one number with lm/W behind it, so that much is known — and what followed is not lost, the free-text round reads `Ra80` into Kleurweergave. Levensduur is taken from the family as written (`>50.000 L80B10 Ta25`) — an L-figure only means something with its B-figure and its Ta beside it, so it is not reduced to a number of hours. On the reference side, **`Ø` is what marks a diameter**, and a diameter belongs in Afmetingen — as a fragment (`Ø226`), behind a label (`Afmetingen: Ø226 x 80(H)`, `Buitenmaat: Ø150x80(H)`) or via `_diameter`. A bare number is never read as a size; that can be anything. The **degree sign** marks the beam angle the same way — as a fragment (`90°`, `60-90°`) or behind a label — with one thing it must never swallow: `°C`/`°F`/`°K` is an ambient or colour temperature, which the `$` anchor after the sign already keeps out. The space a supplier's table puts before the sign (`90 °`) is normalised away, so both columns read alike. Two trade distinctions the veldmap has to keep straight: **inbouwdiameter / zaagdiameter / zaagmaat is the hole**, not the fixture, so `_cutout` is matched before `_diameter` — reverse them and a cut-out reads back as the outside size; and the directly-read `afmetingen` is applied **before** the cut-out is appended, otherwise the outside size silently drops as soon as a cut-out is read too (`Ø226 — cut-out Ø200-210` becomes just the cut-out). A cut-out that is a range keeps its range (`zaagTekst()`); only a single bare number is rounded. The family picker searches **what inside sales actually types** (`zoekFamilies()`): the series plus what she wants of it — `sigma dali`, `sigma 60x60`, `mondial verdiept facet`. Since the family is the series, `DALI` and `60x60` are no longer in the family name, and when every word had to hit the family text those searches all found **0 families** (measured: `sigma dali`, `sigma 60x60`, `sigma g2`, `mondial facet`, `plafonniere` without its accent, `1043227 TC DA` with spaces). Now a word may also sit in an **article** of the family (`artikelTekst()`: description, type, colour, optic, execution, generation, dim protocol, driver, size, IP and the article code with its dashes as spaces), but **one article has to cover every word** — `sigma zwart dali` only fits if there is a black DALI article, not one black and another DALI. The matching articles come back as `passend` (null when they are the whole family), the dropdown row says “39 van 117 artikelen passen”, and `kies()` picks within them: exactly one and that is the article (`handmatig`), several and `autoKies()` chooses among them, so the sheet already carries the typed properties. Input and text both go through **`zoeken.js`**, the shared search helper (`zoekNormaal()`: case, accents, `60 x 60`/`60×60`, `4000 K`; `bevatWoord()`: a word with a digit may not start right after another digit, so `15w` does not find 115W; `maatLezingen()`: `600x600` is the 60x60cm and `120x30` the 30x120). **An article number has to match one-to-one**: a word that looks like one (`lijktCode()`: five digits in a row, not ending in a unit) is only ever compared as a code, never as loose text — otherwise `104322` would ride along on 1043227 and the tool would offer an article nobody typed. Codes compare exactly first; only when that fails **anywhere in the catalogue** may the bare key without dashes and spaces (`codeSleutel()`) match, so `1043227TCDA` finds 1043227-TC-DA — but three pairs in this catalogue differ only by a dash and are different articles (`4030-P-HO`/`4030-PHO`, 12900 against 12750 lm), so `artikelVoorWoord()` only points at an article when that bare key is unique (`codeIndex()`/`codeKlopt()`); `4030pho` brings both into view and points at neither. A code needs a digit but not five of them: `4030-PHO` has four. `artikelVoorWoord()` decides *which* article a typed number points at: an exact `artikelcode` or `barcode` (one article each), never a `basiscode` (several versions — it brings the family into view and you choose). The whole input is tried as one code first, so `1043227 TC DA` means 1043227-TC-DA and not the bare 1043227 that is also in it, and the pointed article must be among `passend`. Only a word containing a digit counts as an article number (otherwise “wit” would point at the first article in the family). An exact article sorts above an exact basiscode, which sorts above every name match. **A typo gets a suggestion, never a silent correction**: when a search finds nothing, `zoekSuggestie()` replaces each word that occurs nowhere by the closest series word (edit distance 1 for short words, 2 for longer ones; `besteSuggestie()`), and offers “Bedoelde je: sigma?” only if that corrected input finds something — never for a number, because a near miss on a number is the wrong article. The empty list for a number says the number has to match exactly, rather than “geen familie gevonden”, which would send you hunting for a typo in the name. `tools/controleer-logica.mjs` runs `zoekFamilies()` over the real catalogue with every query from that measurement as a proof. Unlike the rest of the suite it is *generated*, so don't edit it by hand — the sources live in `vergelijker/` (see `vergelijker/README.md`):

- `vergelijker/index-template.html` — the tool itself; this is what you change.
- `vergelijker/data/armaturen.json` — the product data, injected into the template at the `/*__ARMATUREN_DATA__*/ null` placeholder.
- `vergelijker/HANDLEIDING-familie-toevoegen.md` — the step-by-step for adding product data on a laptop, written for the person doing it rather than for a developer. Keep it in step with the scripts; it names their exact output.
- `python3 vergelijker/bouw-tool.py` regenerates `vergelijking.html` from those two. `bouw-data.py` only needs re-running when a new export lands in `vergelijker/data/bron/`; `armaturen.json` is committed, so the tool can be rebuilt without them. **A raw price list never goes into that folder** — this repo is public and those sheets carry purchase prices, tiers and margins in columns `bouw-data.py` ignores but git would not. `vergelijker/knip-export.py` cuts out the only three columns the tool reads (`artikelcode`, `merk`, `omschrijving`) and writes `data/bron/<naam>.csv`. **`data/bron/catalogus.csv` is the whole Pragmalux range in those three columns** (5062 rows, committed), and `catalogusfamilies()` derives the families from it: everything in front of the first spec token is the name, and articles sharing a **normalised** name (`familiesleutel()`) are one family — 219 families out of 3524 articles with nothing typed by hand (measured on the current catalogue; see the family-key rules below for how it got from ~400 to that). A row is dropped when its **name** carries an accessory word or when it states neither a luminous flux nor a wattage. That word list is in two halves: `TOEBEHOREN_HARD` (frame, component, beugel, profiel, ring, …) is never a fixture, while `TOEBEHOREN_ZACHT` (reflector, lens, kap, driver, frame) also describes a fixture's optic or build — `Railspot Piccolo 15W 24D Reflector` is a loose reflector but `Bandrasterarmatuur Optic matte reflector 147x1570mm 26-36W 3550-4700lm` is a fixture, and `LED Frame Paneel Conto 30x120cm 20-40W 2300-5000lm` is a panel *with* a frame while `Essence G2 licht verdiepte frame PC Ø110` is a loose one — so those only drop a row that does not state **both** a flux and a wattage. Which words belong in which half is measured, not guessed: across the whole catalogue exactly 77 rows state both and are still dropped on a hard word, and they are 72 Conto panels (why `frame` moved to the soft half) and 5 genuine `component LED module` rows (why `component` did not). A third test catches what neither half can see, because the word falls **outside** the name: a row that mentions an accessory and states **no light output at all** — neither a luminous flux nor a colour temperature — is that accessory. `Pragmalux LED Paneel Sigma G2 20W Universele LED Driver (DALI2, 1-10V, Push-dim)` is the driver for the panel, not the panel, and it says `LED Paneel` in its own name so nothing else stops it. Widening the name zone is not the answer: 60 genuine panels are sold `Excl. LED Driver` and would fall with it. What those 60 have is a light figure, and a driver or a loose reflector has none. Measured over the whole catalogue it removes 18 rows and all 18 are accessories — drivers for the Sigma, Easy and Fora ranges, LED-strip drivers, and the Railspot Piccolo's loose reflectors, which stood in this file as the worked example of what should drop but never actually did, because `Reflector` sits behind the wattage and therefore outside the name. Two of those drivers are named after the series alone (`Sigma G2 20W …`) and had made a second family called `Sigma` beside the 117 real Sigma panels — exactly the fragmentation the family key exists to prevent. A dropped row that does state a flux is reported line by line, because that is the one that might have been a fixture. `SOORTEN` — the fixture kinds the search bar matches on, so that typing `downlight` finds them without knowing the family name — gained the ones the price list uses but the table did not know: batten, wallwasher, spiegelarmatuur, breedstraler, lowbay, montagebalk, spotlight, wandlamp, straatverlichting. That took the kept articles without any recognised kind from 142 to 27. `knip-export.py --aanvullen` merges a second export into an existing `data/bron/*.csv`: article codes already there keep their row untouched, only new codes are appended, so a supplementary list is a purely additive git diff. It also skips a code it has already written **in the same run** — one supplied export listed 57 of its rows twice, and without that the article landed twice in its family. **The catalogue is not single-brand**: it carries Pragmalux, Interlight, White Label, SLV and DLC, so the grouping key is `(merk, familiesleutel(naam))` — the same spot sold under two labels is not one family, and the Leverancier row would otherwise show whichever brand happened to win. Where that leaves two families wearing the same name, and only there, the brand is put in front of the name; adding it everywhere would print "Pragmalux" 400 times without distinguishing anything. `merkVoorrang()` then drops the genuine cross-brand duplicates by `MERK_VOORRANG` (Pragmalux first): a row counts as a duplicate only when its description **without the brand word** is identical to another's — deliberately strict, because two brands sharing a product name are not duplicates (a Pragmalux Inbouwspot Orion and an Interlight 3-Fase Track Orion are different fixtures). Each drop is reported by article number. Measured on this catalogue: Pragmalux and Interlight overlap in nothing at all; the only duplicates are six Pragmalux/White Label GU10 spots. **The family is the series, not the version.** Someone who types "sigma" wants one choice and then narrows it, not six families that are all Sigma — so everything that describes a *version* of the same series comes out of the key and comes back as a dropdown: the size (`MAAT_IN_NAAM`), the generation (`GENERATIE`, G2/G3/V2), the optic (`OPTIEK_SLEUTEL`) and the S/M/L format (`FORMAAT`, stripped before the punctuation is flattened, or `Lumio-S` would already read as a stray `s`). A **bare number** in the name only goes when the article itself states that same dimension (`matenVan()`): `Mado 195` beside `Ø195 Buitenmaat` is a size and therefore a version, but the 5 and the 7 in `Retroline RIDI-VLSG-5` and `-7` are the fixture the module fits and have to stay apart — `controleer-families.py` asserts both, through `SAMEN` and `APART`. `toonnaam()` strips the same things from the **real** name rather than from the key, so the capitals survive: `LED Paneel 60x60cm Sigma G2` becomes `LED Paneel Sigma`. The sheet still names the precise article, because `typeVan()` prefers the variant's own derived type — the picker shows the series, the document shows the article, and the PDF regression came out word for word identical across this change. That took 414 families to 218 and the one-article families from 122 to 38. Merging generations means a hand-written overlay now covers both: the Essence Classic block's IP66/IK08/levensduur reaches its two G2 articles as well as its ten G3 ones, and its `naam` had to lose the `G3` it used to carry. **The raw name is not the key.** The price list writes the same series four ways, and each one used to become its own family: case (`LED TL waterdicht armatuur typhoon` beside `… Typhoon`), language (`110x1195mm recessed` beside `1195x110mm inbouw`), unit (`11x120cm opbouw` beside `1195x110mm opbouw`) and typo (`Mado 240 Matt`). `familiesleutel()` folds all four: lower-case, `VERTAAL` maps the English mounting words and `matt`→`mat`, sizes go through `maatSleutel()` which rounds to whole centimetres and sorts largest-first (so 1195 mm and 120 cm meet), a leading `LED` drops, and `x/x` collapses. It also strips `VARIANTWOORDEN` — the qualifiers that describe the *article* rather than the series: DALI2, D4i, Casambi, SF Driver, step-dim, a supply-voltage range, a beam angle. `Venus G2 DALI2` is the DALI version of Venus G2, and the article code says so too (`1000152-DA` beside `1000152`). Anything that makes it a *different fixture* — another optic (`Mado 195 Darklight`), another diameter, a built-in PIR — deliberately stays in the key. `toonnaam()` then picks which spelling the family wears: the one most articles use, ties going to the shortest (the stripped qualifier is what made it longer) and then alphabetical, so the result does not depend on file order. Two forms of size wording are normalised in the description itself before the name is taken (`normaliseerOmschrijving()`), because the name zone stops at a bracket: `(LxW)`/`(Width X Length)` is a label saying which size follows, not a spec, and `1545mm length x 165mm width` is `1545x165mm` spelled out. Without those, every bandrasterarmatuur written that way collapsed into one nameless family. **The dim protocol is read from the description, not from the code suffix** (`DIM_IN_TEKST`): the suffix has 196 forms in this catalogue of which the table covers seventeen, while the text says `DALI2` outright — and `DRIVERS[""]`, being all `None`, is filtered out before the update so it cannot wipe what the description gave. What the suffix *is* good for is the driver's make, and four forms were missing from `DRIVERS` while the description named them outright: `-OS` (+Osram Driver) and the three Philips variants that carry the driver's current instead of stopping at `-PH` (`-PH-350`, `-PH-700`, `-PH30-700`). Those 140 articles had an empty Driver field, the Sigma's 45 Osram versions among them. `vergelijker/controleer-families.py` is the measuring stick for all of this: `PROEVEN` are hand-checked cases with an expected family count, `SAMEN` asserts that two spellings land in the *same* family (a count alone does not prove that), and it prints how many families have one or two articles. A family of one is not automatically wrong — a bandrasterarmatuur is made to size, and 118 of them genuinely are one-offs. `families.json` became the **hand-written overlay**: a block points at one or more derived families with `namen` and merges in what the price list cannot say (IP, IK, UGR, `cri_min`, levensduur, `presenter`). Its `naam` is only applied when the block covers exactly one family — otherwise a 30x120 and a 60x60 panel would end up with the same name — and a block matching nothing is reported rather than silently ignored. A block that still carries `bron_excel` keeps its own export, as before. The description is one line carrying the whole technical specification, and `lees_omschrijving()` in `bouw-data.py` is what reads it out — power (including the switchable `12W/18W` form), colour temperature as written (`3000K-6000K 3-CCT`), luminous flux, the literal size phrase (`Ø217 Buitenmaat - Gatmaat Ø65-185`, so a range survives where the bare `zaagmaat_mm` the fit check needs cannot hold one) the UGR and the colour rendering as written (`UGR<19`, `CRI>90` — 2190 and 1469 of the catalogue's rows state one), and the fixture's own name, being everything in front of the first spec token. **A range is sorted before it is stored**: the price list writes a dipswitch setting high-to-low (`75-22W`, `13000-3800lm`), which left `min` above `max` on 655 articles and made filtering on luminous flux match nothing. The colour temperature writes its `K` once at the end of a chain (`3000-4000K`, which used to yield just `4000K`) and sometimes uses a thousands point (`3.000/4.000/5.700K`), so `CCT_TEKST` accepts both. On the sheet those beat what `families.json` says about the whole series, with one exception shaped like `ipVan()`: `typeVan()` keeps the family name while it already contains what the description says, so `Essence Classic G3` does not overwrite `LED TL Waterdicht Armatuur Essence Classic G3 IP66` but `LED Inbouw/Opbouw Downlight Luna G2` does overwrite `LED Downlight Luna G2`. A description naming two mounting methods (`Inbouw/Opbouw`) sets no `uitvoering` at all, so the family's list stays. **What sits behind the IP rating is the article, not the family**, and the Mondial is the worked example: one downlight in four depths (standaard, verdiept, plat, opbouw) plus a 3-fase track version, each with a facet or a hoogglans reflector, each in white and black, and all of them also in DALI — 252 articles under one name. Those words all fall outside the name zone, so they are read as variant fields (`OPTIEK` → `optiek`, `UITVOERINGEN` → `uitvoering`) and shown as narrowing dropdowns through `KENMERKEN` in the tool, not as families. The article code corroborates the reading independently: `WVF1092379` is Wit Verdiept Facet, `ZSGH…` is Zwart Standaard hoogGlans. `KENMERK_AFLEIDING` supplies the two that are not plain fields — `wattage` from `vermogen_w` and `aansturing` from `dimprotocol`. Roughly 60% of the Mondial rows are cord-set variants (`-ST3`, `-GST3`, `-EUR`) and are skipped by `SNOER` as before; the matrix was checked to be complete without them. `bouw-data.py` re-reads the descriptions in `armaturen.json` when an export is missing, so the committed families move with the parser; `.gitignore` keeps `.xlsx` out of that folder and lets `.csv` in. The stripped CSV *belongs* in the repo: it makes `armaturen.json` reproducible by anyone, and being text it lets git show which rows changed between two price-list rounds, which an `.xlsx` cannot. `bouw-data.py` reads both formats and prefers a `.csv` sitting next to the `.xlsx` named in `bron_excel`, so switching over costs no edit in `families.json`.

Like everything else here it stays a single self-contained file that runs from `file://` — the Python scripts are data prep, not a build step for the suite.

`vergelijking.html` also generates its own PDF ("PDF-voorbeeld" in the top bar), in the order: cover → one page per bestekpositie → ARMATUREN divider → each presenter the positions use, once, in order of first use → `achterpaginas`. It reuses the house-style measurements of the installer PDF described in `docs/railtool.md` — the cover, the ARMATUREN divider page, the blue header band with `ruitpatroon`, the grey diagonal footer shape — and since the `pdf-huisstijl.js` refactor it draws with that same shared layer (see "Drawing layer" in `docs/gedeeld.md`); only the page composition is its own. **Which presenter goes behind a position is decided by `presenterVan(p, fam, v)`**, in this order: what was chosen at the position (the "Presenter in het boek" dropdown under the article — a presenter, or `-` for "Geen presenter"), then the family's `presenter` field in `vergelijker/data/families.json` (hand work wins from recognition), then `matchArmGroep()` — the armaturenboek's own recognition, from `armatuur-groepen.js` — on the description of the chosen **article**. The article and not the family, because one family can carry two presenters: the Sigma G2 and the Sigma G2 IP65 are one series in the price list. Without an article it takes the presenter all the family's articles agree on (`familieGroep()`), and otherwise none. Only for Pragmalux families: the presenters are Pragmalux sheets, and an Interlight 3-Fase Track Orion is not the Pragmalux Orion. Before this only the hand-written field counted, which covered 3 of the 209 Pragmalux families (47 articles), so nearly every PDF ended in "Nog geen presenter voor …"; now 152 families and 2760 of 3473 articles have one. `controleer-logica.mjs` asserts that lower bound, that no presenter's kind contradicts the family's `armatuurtype` (no panel behind a downlight presenter — the check was confirmed to fire on the matcher from before the kind rule, on Paneel Clean → Highbay Clean), and that no other brand gets one.
